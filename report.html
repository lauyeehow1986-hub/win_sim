🔬 Mathematical Insight: Uniform Random vs. Human Distributions

When passwords are drawn from a pure, uniform cryptographic random distribution across $000000000$ to $999999999$:

  • Every candidate has equal probability ($P = 10^{-9}$).
  • According to the No Free Lunch Theorem for Search and Optimization (Wolpert & Macready), no heuristic or permutation ordering can outperform standard sequential search in mathematical expectation over uniform entropy.
  • Both algorithms average almost exactly 500 million attempts (~60 minutes), resulting in a near 50/50 statistical split (48.8% vs. 51.2%).

⚡ Where Jev Dominates: The Human Pattern Cohort

In the real world—the exact focus of Hive Systems' password security awareness research—human beings do not generate uniform random numbers. People pick birthdays, calendar dates, repetitions, sequences, and numpad patterns.

Key Finding: On human-structured passwords, Jev's System 1 calibrated priors solve the target in seconds, while Sequential Brute Force requires up to almost 2 hours.

🏁 Summary & Conclusions

1. Pure Random Security: A truly random 9-digit PIN provides strong theoretical defense against both sequential brute force and AI-guided priors, forcing an attacker to evaluate ~500 million keys on average (~60 minutes on high-end hardware).

2. Human Bias Vulnerability: Passwords based on dates, repeated keys, or numpad shapes reduce the effective search space from 1 billion combinations down to thousands or hundreds, enabling calibrated System 1 models to crack them in under 2 seconds.
